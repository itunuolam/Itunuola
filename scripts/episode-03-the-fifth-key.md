## CHARACTER BIBLE

**Main Character — Maya Whitfield**
30-year-old woman, dark curly hair pulled back into a low bun, hazel eyes, warm brown skin tone, slim build. Wears a fitted charcoal blazer over a cream blouse, dark tailored trousers, and a thin brown leather watch on her left wrist. Composed, observant expression — the kind of stillness that comes from working carefully with delicate objects for a living.

> Repeat these exact details in every AI image prompt where Maya appears, to preserve visual consistency.

**Recurring Object — The Keys**
Small brass skeleton-style keys, roughly 2 inches long, each with a small numbered paper tag tied to the loop with string. Older keys show dulled, worn brass. The newest key is noticeably shinier with visible fresh machining marks.

**Supporting Character — The Locksmith**
Elderly man, gray beard, wire-rimmed glasses, worn tan work apron. Appears briefly in Scene 4–5 only, seen mostly from the side or in profile behind a counter — never a focal point.

## QUALITY CHECK

1. Strong first 5 seconds? ✅ A freshly cut key arriving on a five-year anniversary ritual immediately signals something is wrong.
2. Is there a mystery? ✅ Who is sending the keys, and how, if her father has been dead for years.
3. Does the story escalate? ✅ New key → locksmith reveals a standing order → ledger proves it predates his death → paperwork reveals ten keys total → storage unit discovery.
4. Unexpected ending? ✅ No fraud, no ghost — her father, upon learning his illness was terminal, prepaid and pre-arranged a decade of deliveries so he could still mark years he knew he'd miss.
5. Can every scene be generated visually? ✅ Every scene is a grounded, single realistic composition — keys, drawers, ledgers, a storage unit.
6. Is the main character visually consistent? ✅ Same hair, eyes, skin tone, blazer, and watch repeated in every prompt.
7. Does it avoid clichés? ✅ No inheritance-lawyer trope, no villain, no jump scare — a quiet act of posthumous devotion instead.
8. Would someone want to watch until the end? ✅ The "she's only halfway there" escalation and the emotional reveal both reward watching to the end.
