# THE FINAL CLUE — Episode 01: "The Birthday Photos"
## VOICE-OVER SCRIPT

**Pacing target:** ~135–150 words per minute, cinematic restraint (slower than casual speech, with deliberate pauses — not rushed narration)
**Voice direction:** Calm, low-register, slightly weary — like someone recounting a memory they're still processing, not a horror-trailer announcer. Let the pauses (—) breathe. Volume and pace tighten slightly through Escalation, then soften and slow for the Reveal and Final Line.
**Total spoken word count:** ~140 words across 85 seconds (short on purpose — the images carry weight the words don't need to repeat)

> Of a moment that hadn't happened yet.

*(Delivery: flat, matter-of-fact on the first line — let the second line land as the hook.)*

*(Delivery: slow down hard here. Each sentence gets its own breath. This is the emotional turn, not a jump scare.)*

>
> The scariest photographs weren't of her future.

*(Delivery: softest, slowest line in the piece. Let the final sentence trail rather than punch — this closes on quiet acceptance, not shock.)*

## FULL SCRIPT (continuous, for read-through / timing rehearsal)

Every year, on her birthday, a photograph appeared under Mara's door. Of a moment that hadn't happened yet.

She lived alone. Quiet mornings. Coffee, silence, and a birthday, three days away.

In a box, in her closet — years of these photos. Every single one had already come true.

This year's photo was different. Her — in a hospital bed. Crying. A birthday she didn't recognize.

She checked the hallway camera. No one had been there. The photo had just... appeared.

Behind her bookshelf, she found something she'd hidden from herself. Letters from a clinic. Weeks old. Still unopened.

That night, she drove somewhere she didn't remember choosing — and ended up outside a hospital she'd never seen before. Or so she thought.

Inside, a nurse handed her a file. And on her phone... the same photo. Taken three weeks ago. By her. She just didn't remember.

This time, she didn't look away.

The scariest photographs weren't of her future. They were of a past... she'd refused to remember.

---

## PRODUCTION NOTES

- **Silence is a tool.** Leave the last 3–4 seconds of Scene 9 with no narration at all — just the on-screen text card and ambient room tone, so the final line has room to land before the video ends.
- **Do not narrate what the image already shows.** The script never describes what's on screen (e.g., it doesn't say "she picked up the photo" — the visual does that). It only adds what the image can't: her interior state, time context, and the details connecting scenes.
- **Music cue suggestion:** low sustained drone under Setup, a single rising string line entering at the Strange Event, dropping out almost entirely for the Reveal so the nurse's line and the narration are the only sound, then a soft resolving note under the Final Line.
